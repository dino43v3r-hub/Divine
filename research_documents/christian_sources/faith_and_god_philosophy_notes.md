Key ideas:

Faith can be understood as more than bare belief. Philosophical discussions often describe faith through several overlapping components: trust, belief, hope, commitment, action, and a way of interpreting reality.

In Christian theology, faith is often personal and relational: faith is not only belief that God exists, but trust in God, response to grace, and loyalty expressed through life and action.

The question of reason is central. Some models treat faith as knowledge, some as belief supported by evidence, some as trust that goes beyond full proof, and some as a venture or commitment that shapes how a person lives.

The classical Christian idea of God includes divine attributes such as goodness, wisdom, eternity, unity, omnipotence, omniscience, mercy, justice, and love. Aquinas approaches God through structured questions about existence, simplicity, perfection, goodness, infinity, knowledge, will, and providence.

Creation is not only an event but a claim about dependence, order, intelligibility, and meaning. If creation is ordered by divine wisdom, then patterns in nature, reason, moral life, and worship may be studied as clues rather than random fragments.

Religious epistemology asks whether belief in God is rational, what counts as evidence, whether experience can justify belief, and whether trust in God can be intellectually responsible.
