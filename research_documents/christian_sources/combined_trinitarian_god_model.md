# Combined Christian Model Of God

This note combines the prior God, Jesus, and Holy Spirit analysis into one Christian theological model.

## Core Claim

In Christianity, God is not best represented as an abstract supreme being only. The Christian source pattern is Trinitarian: one God known as Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.

This matters for the divine-pattern project because each Trinitarian relation connects to a different layer of human meaning and behavior.

## Father

The Father is associated with source, creation, providence, wisdom, authority, love, mercy, justice, and the gift of life.

Behavioral relationships:

- Creation gives humans a world that can be studied as ordered and meaningful.
- Providence frames life as held within divine care.
- Moral authority places human behavior under accountability.
- Fatherhood shapes identity, belonging, dependence, trust, and obedience.

## Son

The Son, Jesus Christ, is associated with incarnation, revelation, redemption, cross, resurrection, discipleship, kingdom, forgiveness, and hope.

Behavioral relationships:

- Incarnation connects God with embodied human life.
- The cross connects love with sacrifice, forgiveness, suffering, and moral reversal.
- Resurrection connects faith with hope, courage, endurance, and new creation.
- Discipleship turns belief into imitation, practice, humility, mercy, service, and enemy-love.
- Lordship creates allegiance and reorders identity.

## Holy Spirit

The Holy Spirit is associated with divine presence, inspiration, comfort, conviction, empowerment, sanctification, gifts, unity, and transformation.

Behavioral relationships:

- Presence connects worship with comfort, assurance, and nearness.
- Conviction connects moral awareness with repentance.
- Sanctification connects faith with gradual transformation.
- Gifts connect personal ability with communal service.
- Unity connects individual experience with the life of the church.

## Unified Pattern

The Father creates and sends.

The Son reveals and redeems.

The Holy Spirit indwells and transforms.

Together, this creates a Christian behavior pattern:

1. Humans are created by God.
2. Humans are morally accountable to God.
3. Humans experience disorder through sin, suffering, estrangement, and death.
4. Jesus reveals God's character and opens redemption through incarnation, cross, and resurrection.
5. Faith becomes trust in Christ, not only belief in an idea.
6. The Spirit applies transformation through conviction, comfort, sanctification, gifts, and communal life.
7. Worship, prayer, scripture, sacrament, service, and moral practice become repeated patterns that train identity and behavior.

## Relationship To Science And Human Behavior

Scientific and social-scientific research cannot prove the Trinity. It can, however, study the human patterns associated with Trinitarian belief:

- Father-language may relate to identity, trust, obedience, moral accountability, and belonging.
- Jesus-centered discipleship may relate to imitation, forgiveness, sacrifice, humility, service, suffering, and hope.
- Holy-Spirit language may relate to inner transformation, spiritual experience, communal unity, courage, and moral formation.
- Worship and ritual may embody and transmit these patterns over time.

## Divine Pattern Hypothesis

The Christian pattern of God is relational, not merely conceptual.

The strongest Christian divine-pattern hypothesis is:

God is understood as the source of being and moral order; Jesus Christ is the embodied revelation and redemptive pattern; the Holy Spirit is the active presence that transforms persons and communities.

This hypothesis should be tested across scripture, creeds, theology, worship, history, psychology, anthropology, neuroscience, and lived Christian practice.
