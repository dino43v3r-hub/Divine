# Research Governance Workflow

Type: research method protocol
Source status: active governance layer

This workflow converts broad pattern discovery into auditable theological
research. It keeps the project from moving too quickly from recurrence to
confidence.

## Confidence Tiers

### candidate_lead

A source or observation may help route attention, but it does not strengthen a
claim.

Use this tier for:

- auto-imported cloud candidates,
- summaries without original-source review,
- media without caption, transcript, or direct observation,
- sources missing evidence or counter-reading.

### developing_evidence

A source has evidence and at least one counter-reading, but it is still missing
one or more promotion controls.

Use this tier when:

- the source is real and relevant,
- the reviewer can say what it supports,
- rival explanations are visible,
- interpretation, analogy, discernment, failure condition, or practical use is
  still incomplete.

### reviewed_evidence_ready

A source has all promotion-required controls and is ready for human confidence
review. This is not automatic promotion.

Use this tier only when the review separates:

- evidence,
- interpretation,
- discernment,
- analogy,
- practical use,
- counter-reading,
- failure condition,
- machine-label boundary.

### analogy_only

A source or concept illuminates a theological idea but does not support proof
language.

Use this tier especially for:

- science and quantum language,
- mathematics and beauty,
- music and art,
- comparative motifs,
- symbolic or literary resonance.

### do_not_strengthen_claim

A source may still be useful as a warning, pressure test, or rejected route, but
it must not raise confidence.

Use this tier when:

- domain limits are exceeded,
- the source is too remote from the claim,
- the claim would become coercive or harmful,
- rival explanations fit better,
- the material risks flattening another tradition.

## Anti-Confirmation-Bias Rule

Before confidence rises, the reviewer must name at least one serious rival
explanation that could explain the same signal without the Divine Pattern claim.

Common rival explanations:

- shared human cognition,
- social formation,
- trauma response,
- ritual structure,
- literary genre,
- political power,
- biological adaptation,
- selection bias,
- curated source effects,
- institutional self-protection,
- ordinary pattern perception.

## Failure-Condition Rule

Every attractive pattern must answer:

```text
What would weaken, narrow, or reject this claim?
```

Common weakening cases:

- unresolved suffering,
- injustice without repair,
- spiritual abuse,
- institutional success with moral failure,
- science language exceeding source scope,
- other traditions explained unfairly,
- practical fruit becoming coercive,
- a rival explanation fitting the evidence better.

## Suffering And Abuse First-Class Test

Do not treat suffering, abuse, or failed repair as edge cases. They are primary
tests for theological pattern claims.

A claim must be downgraded when it:

- explains tragedy too quickly,
- asks victims to absorb harm for the sake of a neat pattern,
- uses forgiveness language without protection or justice,
- treats later good as if it cancels the wound,
- protects a leader, institution, or movement from accountability.

## Source Promotion Steps

1. Identify the claim affected.
2. Read the original source or reviewed section.
3. Record what the source actually supports.
4. Record the Christian interpretation separately.
5. Add at least one serious rival explanation.
6. Add analogy limits.
7. Add failure conditions.
8. Add practical-theology boundaries.
9. Check pastoral and ethical harm.
10. Check the priestly discernment gate: pastoral safety, ecclesial review,
    sacramental/liturgical grounding, and promotion restraint.
11. Decide whether confidence rises, stays provisional, weakens, or is blocked.

## Priestly Discernment Gate

Before a claim is used publicly, devotionally, or pastorally, it must answer:

- Would this be safe beside a hospital bed, at a funeral, in confession, or with
  a person harmed by religious authority?
- Does this protect vulnerable people before protecting leaders, institutions,
  systems, or the attractiveness of the pattern?
- Does this preserve lament, mystery, justice, and repair?
- What ecclesial review is required before public use?
- How does this remain accountable to baptism, Eucharist, confession, anointing,
  funerals, the church year, or daily prayer without reducing worship to
  symbolism?
- Why should this claim remain research-only, analogy-only, developing,
  reviewed-ready, or blocked rather than being overpromoted?

## Report Language Rule

Published language should preserve the tier:

- `candidate_lead`: "This source suggests a question for review."
- `developing_evidence`: "This source may support the claim, but review remains incomplete."
- `reviewed_evidence_ready`: "This source is ready for human confidence review."
- `analogy_only`: "This illuminates the claim but does not prove it."
- `do_not_strengthen_claim`: "This source should not strengthen the claim."

## Practical-Theology Gate

Before a claim is used devotionally, pastorally, or publicly, ask:

```text
Does this help people become more truthful, loving, humble, just, worshipful,
patient, and faithful?
```

If not, the claim may remain an interesting observation, but it has not become
practical theology.
