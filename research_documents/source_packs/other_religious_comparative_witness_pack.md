# Source Pack: Other Religious Comparative Witness

Source status: reviewed source pack

Claim boundary: Christian theology may compare wisdom, longing, mercy, ritual,
lament, moral practice, and possible prevenient grace across religious
traditions, but other traditions must be studied on their own terms.

Evidence: primary texts, ritual practices, expert commentary, living-community
interpretation, historical contact, testimony, and counter-readings from within
each tradition.

Interpretation: Christian theology may ask how shared human patterns relate to
creation, conscience, longing, or grace, but this remains interpretation rather
than proof.

Discernment: comparison must be accountable to humility, neighbor-love,
doctrinal clarity, source expertise, and harmed-community concerns.

Analogy: shared practices may arise from common human needs, social formation,
ritual stability, ecological conditions, memory, or ordinary pattern perception.

Practical use: learn, listen, serve neighbors, speak truth respectfully, and
avoid triumphalism.

Failure condition: reject any comparison that flattens difference, extracts
sacred material, treats another tradition as a prop, or hides Christian
misconduct in interreligious history.
