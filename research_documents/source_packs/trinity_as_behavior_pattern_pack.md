# Source Pack: Trinity-As-Behavior Pattern

Source status: reviewed source pack

Claim boundary: Trinity-As-Behavior may describe how Christian doctrine forms
practice through receiving creation as gift, following Christ, and testing
Spirit-led transformation. It must preserve Father, Son, and Holy Spirit as
theological persons, not interchangeable symbols.

Evidence:

- Matthew 28:19, John 14-17, Romans 8, 2 Corinthians 13:14, Ephesians, baptismal
  language, creeds, and worship texts support Trinitarian practice.
- Theologian notes from Tertullian, Nicaea, Gregory Nazianzen, Augustine,
  Aquinas, Rahner, LaCugna, Zizioulas, and Pentecostal discernment name
  continuity and disagreement.
- History notes on councils, schism, mission, abuse, and reform test doctrine
  under practice.

Interpretation: Christian theology reads creation, redemption, and
sanctification as distinct but inseparable divine action.

Discernment: experiences, gifts, authority claims, and communal practices must
be tested by love, truth, holiness, humility, unity, justice, and fruit.

Analogy: sociology of group identity, ritual studies, psychology of belonging,
and comparative triadic symbols may illuminate behavior but cannot replace
doctrine.

Practical use: receive life as gift, follow Christ concretely, and test
Spirit-led change by accountable love and service.

Failure condition: reject any version that collapses the Trinity into one role,
divides God into three gods, treats the Spirit as group energy, or justifies
spiritual control.
