# Source Pack: Cross And Reversal Pattern

Source status: reviewed source pack

Claim boundary: the Cross And Reversal pattern may describe God's revelation in
cruciform humility, mercy, judgment of violent power, forgiveness, and
resurrection hope. It must not romanticize suffering or protect abusers.

Evidence:

- Passion narratives, Isaiah servant songs, Psalm 22, Philippians 2,
  1 Corinthians 1-2, Romans 5-8, Hebrews, and resurrection witness support the
  Christian claim.
- Historical notes on Roman crucifixion, slavery, lynching, genocide, spiritual
  abuse, and civil-rights witness keep the cross under victim-centered pressure.
- Theologian notes from Luther, Bonhoeffer, Cone, Williams, Romero, Sobrino,
  trauma theology, and liberation theology sharpen the boundaries.

Interpretation: Christian theology reads the cross as God's judgment on violent
power and God's self-giving mercy, fulfilled in resurrection hope.

Discernment: cross-language must be tested by whether it protects victims,
tells truth, seeks justice, and refuses coercive forgiveness.

Analogy: literary reversal, martyr sociology, moral-injury repair, and social
psychology of sacrifice can illuminate the pattern but cannot prove it.

Practical use: practice truth-telling, humility, forgiveness with boundaries,
justice for victims, and hope that does not deny wounds.

Failure condition: reject any version that asks harmed people to accept ongoing
injury, hides abuse, praises victimhood, or uses sacrifice language to protect
power.
