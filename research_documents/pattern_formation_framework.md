# Pattern Formation Framework

Type: research method
Source status: active formation rule

The Divine project should keep searching for patterns. Pattern detection is the
discovery engine. It asks, "Where does this language or structure appear?"
Pattern formation is the second movement. It asks, "Now that a pattern has been
found, how is it shaped by scripture, tested by life, clarified by scholarship,
compared across cultures, and practiced by people?"

Do not remove pattern search. Route detected patterns into formation.

## Formation Chapter Template

Each major pattern should become a chapter with six movements:

1. Human problem: the ordinary human experience that makes the pattern matter.
2. Biblical and theological grounding: scripture, doctrine, tradition, and
   primary sources that give the pattern its Christian shape.
3. Scholarly conversation: theologians, languages, history, psychology, science,
   philosophy, and methodological questions that refine the claim.
4. Cross-cultural and interreligious listening: what other peoples, traditions,
   stories, languages, and practices reveal without being flattened.
5. Pressure test: suffering, injustice, abuse, failed repair, counterarguments,
   and misuse conditions that could weaken or revise the pattern.
6. Practical response: what a person or community can practice today with
   humility, truth, love, justice, worship, and hope.

## Growth Rule

A pattern does not mature because it has many word matches. It matures when it
can answer:

- What real human need does this address?
- Which primary texts actually ground it?
- Which scholars or traditions disagree?
- Which source lanes are still thin?
- Which pressure tests would force revision?
- What daily practice follows without coercion or overclaim?

## Routing Rule

Detected pattern -> candidate pattern -> formation chapter -> reviewed pattern.

A detected pattern remains useful as a discovery lead. It becomes a candidate
when it has enough repeated signal to name. It becomes a formation chapter when
the report adds human problem, biblical grounding, scholarly conversation,
cross-cultural listening, pressure tests, and practical response. It becomes a
reviewed pattern only after source review and counter-readings.

## Report Rule

Reader-facing reports should put formation before raw data. They should begin
with human meaning, then show scholarly care, then give the evidence map. Raw
counts belong in research-detail sections, not in the opening paragraphs.
