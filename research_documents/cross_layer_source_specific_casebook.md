# Cross-Layer Source-Specific Casebook

Type: cross-layer synthesis notes
Source status: source-specific case expansion

This casebook links concrete source lanes across visual art, history, language,
biblical-language study, all-texts comparison, and psychology. It exists to
prevent the project from using broad categories as if they were actual source
coverage.

## Case 1: Crucifixion, Trauma, And Public Witness

- Visual art lane: crucifixion and lament images.
- History lane: public execution, empire, religious authority, and memory.
- Biblical-language lane: cross, suffering, witness, Spirit, and resurrection
  vocabulary must be read in context.
- Psychology lane: trauma, grief, moral injury, and coercive misuse of
  suffering language.
- Pattern signal: suffering, love, injustice, witness, and hope converge.
- Counter-reading: the cross can be misused to tell victims to endure abuse.
- Required revision: no claim about redemptive suffering may be promoted unless
  it protects victims and names injustice.

## Case 2: Translation Of Spirit / Breath / Wind

- World-language lane: terms for breath, wind, life, spirit, and divine agency
  differ across languages.
- Biblical-language lane: Hebrew `ruach` and Greek `pneuma` require literary and
  canonical context.
- All-texts lane: many traditions connect breath, life, and spirit-like
  realities.
- Psychology lane: breath practices affect attention, emotion, and embodied
  experience.
- Pattern signal: life, presence, and transformation are often embodied.
- Counter-reading: shared breath/spirit language does not prove identical
  theology.
- Required revision: distinguish Christian pneumatology from broader breath and
  life symbolism.

## Case 3: Wisdom Across Traditions

- All-texts lane: wisdom literature, proverbs, philosophy, and moral teaching.
- History lane: schools, communities, elders, scribes, monastic practice, and
  public ethics.
- Language lane: wisdom terms carry different social and theological fields.
- Psychology lane: habits, attention, memory, emotion regulation, and moral
  learning.
- Pattern signal: wisdom often joins order, practice, humility, and moral life.
- Counter-reading: shared wisdom may reflect human social learning rather than
  direct Trinitarian evidence.
- Required revision: label broad recurrence as shared human pattern unless
  Christian sources carry the distinct claim.

## Case 4: Gifts Of The Holy Ghost Across Cultures

- Theologian lane: gifts must be ordered by love, fruit, doctrine, and communal
  discernment.
- Human-story lane: testimony can show courage, healing, mercy, prophecy,
  discernment, or service.
- Other-religious lane: similar phenomena may appear in other traditions and
  must be read respectfully.
- Psychology lane: charisma, expectation, group dynamics, trauma, and meaning
  can shape reported experiences.
- Pattern signal: gifts may show God working across peoples and boundaries.
- Counter-reading: not every spectacular experience is from the Holy Ghost.
- Required revision: a claimed gift strengthens the pattern only when it bears
  Christ-centered fruit, love, truth, justice, humility, and accountability.

## Case 5: Dreams, Visions, And Conversion

- Human-story lane: dreams and visions are testimony, not statistics.
- Other-religious lane: different traditions interpret dreams and visions in
  their own ways.
- Psychology lane: dreams can be shaped by memory, stress, longing, trauma,
  symbol, and social context.
- Theologian lane: Christian discernment may ask about Jesus' witness and the
  Holy Ghost's drawing.
- Pattern signal: some testimonies suggest divine initiative before explicit
  Christian vocabulary.
- Counter-reading: anonymous or sensational testimony should not strengthen
  claims.
- Required revision: keep testimony at limited-evidence level until source,
  context, fruit, and counter-readings are reviewed.
