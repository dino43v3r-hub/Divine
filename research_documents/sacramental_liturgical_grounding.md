# Sacramental And Liturgical Grounding

Type: practical theology and worship grounding
Source status: active grounding guide

The Divine Pattern project should be rooted in worship before it is used for
argument. Patterns may help people notice, but the Church's worship forms people
through Scripture, prayer, sacrament, confession, lament, praise, and service.

## Core Boundary

Sacraments and liturgy are not illustrations produced by the pattern system.
They are practices of the worshiping Church. Pattern language may humbly notice
resonance, but it must not reduce baptism, Eucharist, confession, anointing, or
funeral hope to symbols that exist to confirm the project.

## Grounding Questions

- Baptism: Does the claim honor identity received by grace rather than earned by
  intelligence, usefulness, purity, status, or performance?
- Eucharist: Does the claim lead to thanksgiving, communion, self-giving love,
  and dependence on Christ rather than spiritual display?
- Confession and reconciliation: Does the claim tell the truth about sin,
  repentance, mercy, accountability, and repair?
- Anointing and pastoral care: Does the claim comfort without promising what God
  has not promised?
- Funerals and grief: Does the claim hold resurrection hope and lament together?
- Church year: Does the claim learn from waiting, incarnation, repentance,
  passion, resurrection, Pentecost, and ordinary faithfulness?
- Daily prayer: Does the claim become humility, attention, obedience, and love?

## Public Use Rule

Before a divine-pattern claim is used devotionally or publicly, write one short
paragraph explaining how it remains accountable to worship, pastoral care, and
the Church's discernment. If that paragraph cannot be written without
overclaiming, keep the claim as research or analogy only.
