# Priestly Review Protocol

Type: pastoral and ecclesial promotion gate
Source status: active review protocol

This protocol slows the Divine Pattern project before any pattern is used as a
public theological claim, pastoral counsel, devotional guide, apologetic
argument, or source of increased confidence.

## Rule

No pattern becomes pastorally usable merely because the system finds recurrence.
A pattern must pass source review, creedal review, harm review, ecclesial
review, and practical-fruit review.

## Review Sequence

1. Name the claim in plain language.
2. Separate evidence from Christian interpretation.
3. Identify the Scripture, creed, doctrine, or tradition boundary involved.
4. Name at least one rival explanation.
5. Ask whether the claim would be safe in pastoral crisis.
6. Ask whether the claim protects the vulnerable before protecting systems.
7. Ask whether the claim preserves lament, mystery, justice, and repair.
8. Ask whether sacramental or liturgical grounding is possible without reducing
   worship to symbolism.
9. Ask what ecclesial review is needed before public use.
10. Decide whether the claim stays a question, becomes analogy-only, develops
    as evidence, is ready for human review, or must not strengthen the project.

## Pastoral Crisis Test

Before using a pattern in public or pastoral language, ask:

- Would I say this beside a hospital bed?
- Would I say this at a funeral?
- Would I say this to someone harmed by spiritual authority?
- Would I say this in confession or spiritual direction?
- Would I say this to someone experiencing chronic illness, abuse, child loss,
  poverty, displacement, or moral injury?

If the answer is no, the claim may remain a research question, but it should not
be promoted for pastoral use.

## Suffering And Abuse Rule

The cross never licenses abuse. Resurrection hope never cancels lament. Divine
providence never removes the duty to protect, tell the truth, seek justice, and
repair harm where repair is possible.

Downgrade any claim that:

- asks victims to absorb harm for the sake of a beautiful pattern,
- uses forgiveness language without safety and justice,
- explains tragedy before listening to sufferers,
- protects leaders or institutions from accountability,
- treats later good as if it erases the wound.

## Ecclesial Review

The project is not a private revelation engine. Strong claims should be reviewed
by mature Christian readers, pastors, theologians, and source reviewers. Claims
touching other religions, science, trauma, abuse, or medical suffering require
specialist humility and outside review.

## Liturgical Grounding

When a pattern is pastorally promising, ask how it relates to the Church's lived
worship:

- Baptism: Does it honor new life, identity, death and resurrection in Christ?
- Eucharist: Does it deepen thanksgiving, communion, sacrifice, and grace?
- Confession: Does it invite truth, repentance, mercy, and repair?
- Anointing and care for the sick: Does it comfort without guaranteeing an
  outcome?
- Funerals: Does it speak resurrection hope without denying grief?
- Church year: Does it remain accountable to Advent, Christmas, Lent, Easter,
  Pentecost, Ordinary Time, and the discipline of waiting?
- Daily prayer: Does it form attention, humility, love, and obedience?

Liturgical grounding should deepen worship. It should not turn worship into a
data mine for proving claims.

## Promotion Decision

Use these outcomes:

- `research_question_only`: useful to investigate, not to claim.
- `analogy_only`: illuminates, but does not prove.
- `developing_evidence`: source-supported but still constrained.
- `reviewed_evidence_ready`: ready for accountable human review, not automatic
  promotion.
- `do_not_strengthen_claim`: useful as warning, pressure test, or rejected
  route.
