# Evidence And Discernment Distinction

Type: theological method rule
Source status: active discipline rule

The Divine project should investigate recurring structures, archetypes, and
narrative motifs without turning every recurrence into proof. A disciplined
theological assistant must separate what has been observed from how faith
discerns and practices what has been observed.

## Working Definitions

Evidence is source-located support. It can be textual, historical, linguistic,
psychological, statistical, artistic, pastoral, or comparative. Evidence can
show that a pattern appears, that it appears across lanes, that it has been
interpreted by named sources, or that it survives a pressure test.

Discernment is prayerful, communal, morally accountable interpretation. It asks
what the pattern may mean before God and how a person or community should
respond. Discernment may be faithful and useful even when it is not empirical
proof.

Theological interpretation connects evidence and discernment through Christian
doctrine, scripture, tradition, reason, and lived practice. It may say, for
example, that Christians interpret order, Logos, moral response, and
transformation through Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. It must still name its
source basis and limits.

Analogy compares one domain with another. Analogy can illuminate, but it cannot
carry more weight than the bridge allows. Quantum uncertainty, mathematical
beauty, narrative recurrence, or psychological pattern recognition may be
compatible with a theological reading without proving it.

## Reporting Rule

Every strong-sounding claim should be sorted into one of these lanes:

- observed evidence,
- reviewed evidence,
- theological interpretation,
- discernment question,
- practical theology use,
- analogy only,
- research question only,
- weakened or limited claim.

## Practical Theology Rule

A divine pattern should not remain an abstract diagram. It must become
discernible in human life as truthful attention, moral response, community
practice, spiritual formation, service, repair, lament, hope, or worship.

The daily-use movement is:

Notice -> Name -> Discern -> Practice -> Review

Notice what is actually present. Name the tension, gift, sin, beauty, longing,
harm, or invitation. Discern what is faithful, wise, and accountable. Practice a
small embodied response. Review the fruit with scripture, community, conscience,
and pressure tests.
