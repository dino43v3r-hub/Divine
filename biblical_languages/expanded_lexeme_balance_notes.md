# Expanded Lexeme Balance Notes

Source lane: biblical Greek and Hebrew
Source status: source-specific balancing note

This file adds biblical-language depth so the project does not lean on English
pattern words without source-context checks.

## `ruach`

- Range: wind, breath, spirit, disposition, divine Spirit.
- Pattern relevance: life and divine agency can be embodied and dynamic.
- Counter-reading: not every use of `ruach` refers to the Holy Spirit in later
  Christian Trinitarian terms.
- Required filter: literary context, speaker, genre, and canonical reception.

## `pneuma`

- Range: breath, wind, spirit, human spirit, evil spirit, Holy Spirit.
- Pattern relevance: Spirit language connects presence, life, power, witness,
  and transformation.
- Counter-reading: psychological, emotional, or communal energy should not be
  automatically identified with the Holy Ghost.
- Required filter: New Testament context, Septuagint background, and doctrinal
  reception.

## `dikaiosyne`

- Range: righteousness, justice, covenant faithfulness, right relation.
- Pattern relevance: moral transformation is not only private virtue; it can
  include justice and right ordering.
- Counter-reading: righteousness language can be narrowed into individual moral
  performance while ignoring public justice.
- Required filter: Pauline, Matthean, Septuagint, and Hebrew-background uses
  should be distinguished.

## `agape`

- Range: love, charity, self-giving love, covenantal and communal practice.
- Pattern relevance: gifts and knowledge must be ordered by love.
- Counter-reading: love language can become sentimental or coercive when truth
  and justice are absent.
- Required filter: 1 Corinthians 13, Johannine usage, and ethical context.

## Analyzer Rule

Biblical-language evidence should strengthen a claim only when the term's range,
source context, and counter-reading are named.
