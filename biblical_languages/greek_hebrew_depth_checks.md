# Biblical Greek And Hebrew: Depth Checks

Source lane: biblical Greek and Hebrew notes

Biblical Greek, Hebrew, and Aramaic should be used as a depth check, not as a
shortcut to certainty. The app should look for lemma, syntax, semantic range,
translation range, covenant context, genre, and theological caution.

Pattern observation:

- Greek terms such as logos, agape, and pneuma should be read in sentence,
  genre, and historical context.
- Hebrew terms such as hesed, shalom, ruach, and covenant language often carry
  relational, communal, and embodied meaning.
- Original-language study can strengthen a pattern, complicate it, or correct it.
- A theological claim should not rest on a single word when grammar, context, and
  counter-readings point elsewhere.

Cross-layer question:

Does the original-language evidence support the synthesis, or does it expose a
translation-based overclaim?
