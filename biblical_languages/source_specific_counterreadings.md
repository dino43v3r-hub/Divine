# Source-Specific Biblical-Language Counter-Readings

Source lane: biblical Greek and Hebrew
Source status: lexeme and context pressure notes

These notes keep biblical-language work tied to source context rather than
word-study overreach.

## Hebrew `hesed`

- Source contexts: covenant loyalty, mercy, kinship obligation, divine
  steadfastness, and narrative action.
- Pattern signal: love and faithfulness are relational and enacted.
- Counter-reading: `hesed` cannot be reduced to a generic feeling of love or a
  universal kindness principle.
- Revision rule: use `hesed` only with covenant, narrative, and recipient
  context.

## Hebrew `shalom`

- Source contexts: peace, wholeness, welfare, social order, restoration, and
  covenant blessing.
- Pattern signal: divine repair may include communal and material well-being.
- Counter-reading: `shalom` should not be spiritualized while poverty, violence,
  and injustice remain.
- Revision rule: claims about peace must include social and embodied
  conditions.

## Greek `logos`

- Source contexts: speech, reason, account, ordering principle, and Johannine
  Christological usage.
- Pattern signal: meaning, creation, revelation, and the Son can converge in
  Christian reading.
- Counter-reading: Greek philosophical `logos` and John's `Logos` are not
  identical without argument.
- Revision rule: do not use `logos` as abstract order detached from Christology.

## Greek `pneuma`

- Source contexts: breath, wind, spirit, Holy Spirit, life, and divine agency.
- Pattern signal: transformation and presence are not merely ideas.
- Counter-reading: `pneuma` should not be collapsed into psychology, emotion, or
  community mood.
- Revision rule: preserve divine agency, embodiment, and interpretive context.
