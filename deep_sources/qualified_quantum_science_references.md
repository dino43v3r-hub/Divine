# Qualified Quantum And Science References

Area: Quantum And Science Claims
Source Types: Physicist/Primary Science, Peer-Reviewed/Academic, Philosophy Of Science, Counterargument
Source status: source reviewed

These notes strengthen the science guardrail. They do not claim that quantum
physics proves God. They identify qualified sources and counterarguments that
must shape any responsible comparison.

## Primary Science And Physicist-Authored Anchors

- Max Planck: black-body radiation and the quantum hypothesis.
- Albert Einstein: light quanta, relativity, and objections to quantum
  completeness.
- Niels Bohr: complementarity and interpretation of measurement.
- Werner Heisenberg: uncertainty relations and mathematical quantum mechanics.
- Erwin Schrodinger: wave mechanics and the wave equation.
- Paul Dirac: formal quantum mechanics and quantum field theory foundations.
- John Bell: Bell's theorem, locality, and hidden-variable constraints.
- Alain Aspect and later experimental work: tests of Bell inequalities and
  entanglement.
- Richard Feynman: path integrals, quantum electrodynamics, and disciplined
  caution about intuitive pictures.

## Philosophy Of Science Anchors

- Bas van Fraassen: constructive empiricism and the difference between empirical
  adequacy and metaphysical realism.
- Ian Hacking: experiment, realism, and intervention.
- Nancy Cartwright: limits of laws, models, and abstraction.
- Tim Maudlin: philosophy of physics and the cost of interpretation choices.

## Counterarguments And Limits

- Quantum uncertainty does not imply that consciousness creates reality.
- Entanglement does not mean all things are spiritually connected in a vague
  way.
- Measurement problems are physics and philosophy problems before they are
  theology prompts.
- Mathematical elegance is not the same as doctrinal proof.
- Theological comparison should stay at the level of humility, contingency,
  intelligibility, and disciplined wonder unless a much stronger argument is
  supplied.

Use rule:

Any report language about quantum physics should name the relevant concept,
source type, interpretation limits, and counterargument before drawing a
theological analogy.
