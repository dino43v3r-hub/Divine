# Quantum And Science Claim-Limit Source Pairs

Source lane: deep sources
Source status: qualified source and counterargument pairing

This file pairs science source areas with narrow allowed conclusions and
counterarguments. It is designed to keep quantum/science language from becoming
vague theological proof.

## Quantum Measurement

- Qualified source area: physicist-authored quantum measurement literature,
  formal textbooks, and peer-reviewed measurement research.
- Allowed conclusion: measurement is a technical problem in quantum theory with
  multiple interpretations and mathematical formalisms.
- Not allowed: "measurement proves consciousness creates reality" or "quantum
  collapse proves prayer."
- Counterargument: interpretation disagreement may reflect limits of physical
  theory, not evidence for a theological mechanism.

## Uncertainty And Probability

- Qualified source area: Heisenberg uncertainty, wavefunctions, probability
  amplitudes, and statistical predictions in quantum mechanics.
- Allowed conclusion: physical prediction is not always classical
  determinism.
- Not allowed: "uncertainty means anything can happen spiritually."
- Counterargument: probabilistic law is still mathematically constrained.

## Fine-Tuning And Cosmology

- Qualified source area: cosmology, constants, anthropic reasoning,
  multiverse proposals, and philosophy of science.
- Allowed conclusion: fine-tuning raises philosophical questions about
  intelligibility, contingency, and explanation.
- Not allowed: "fine-tuning proves the Trinity."
- Counterargument: selection effects, unknown physics, multiverse hypotheses,
  and necessity explanations must be named.

## Mathematics And Physical Law

- Qualified source area: philosophy of mathematics, physics, symmetry,
  mathematical modeling, and scientific realism/anti-realism debates.
- Allowed conclusion: mathematics is deeply effective in physical description.
- Not allowed: "mathematical beauty proves divine order."
- Counterargument: mathematical success may reflect model selection, human
  cognition, abstraction, or structural features of the world without settling
  theology.

## Neuroscience And Religious Experience

- Qualified source area: cognitive science of religion, neuroscience of
  prayer/ritual, psychology of meaning, and anthropology.
- Allowed conclusion: religious experience has embodied, social, and cognitive
  dimensions.
- Not allowed: "brain correlates prove or disprove God."
- Counterargument: neural correlates do not decide metaphysical truth.

## Analyzer Rule

Every science claim must name the qualified source area, the narrow allowed
conclusion, and at least one counterargument. If it cannot do all three, the
claim should remain a research question.
