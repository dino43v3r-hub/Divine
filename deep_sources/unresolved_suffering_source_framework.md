Title: Unresolved Suffering Source Framework
Area: Unresolved Suffering
Source Types: Scripture/Theology, Pastoral/Clinical, Lived Case, Counterargument

Scripture and theology should include lament, Job, Psalms, Lamentations, the cross, resurrection hope, and theodicy.
The guardrail is that suffering, grief, trauma, chronic pain, silence, and unanswered prayer must not be forced into quick resolution.

Pastoral and clinical care should include trauma-informed counseling, mental health awareness, community care, patient presence, and practical support.
Pastoral theology should ask how a church or community can stay with people in suffering without denial or blame.

Lived case material should include testimony, case study, daily life practice, and community response.
The goal is to see whether the pattern helps people endure honestly, ask for help, receive care, and practice hope without pretending pain is simple.

Counterargument: some suffering does not appear redemptive, and some people experience silence instead of healing.
This does not automatically disprove the divine pattern, but it challenges any triumphalistic version of it.
Limitation: the pattern should say less, listen more, and avoid claiming to explain every wound.
