# Qualified Science Reference Review Notes

Area: Quantum And Science Claims
Source Types: Physicist/Primary Science, Peer-Reviewed/Academic, Philosophy Of Science, Counterargument
Source status: source review map

This file names qualified science sources and counterarguments that should be
reviewed before making science-related pattern claims.

## Physics And Quantum Mechanics

- David J. Griffiths and Darrell F. Schroeter, *Introduction to Quantum
  Mechanics*: useful for standard formalism, measurement, wave functions, and
  probability without mystical overclaim.
- J. J. Sakurai and Jim Napolitano, *Modern Quantum Mechanics*: advanced
  physicist-authored source for formal quantum theory.
- John S. Bell, "On the Einstein Podolsky Rosen Paradox" (1964): important for
  locality, hidden variables, and the limits of simple classical intuitions.
- Simon Kochen and Ernst Specker, "The Problem of Hidden Variables in Quantum
  Mechanics" (1967): important for contextuality.
- Alain Aspect, Philippe Grangier, and Gerard Roger, experimental Bell-test
  work (1980s): important experimental evidence, but not theological proof.
- Eugene Wigner, "The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Mathematics in the Natural
  Sciences" (1960): classic philosophy-of-science prompt, not a proof of
  Christian doctrine.

## Philosophy And Limits

- Scientific realism, instrumentalism, structural realism, naturalism, theism,
  and Platonism can all interpret order and mathematics differently.
- Quantum probability does not prove God, consciousness, prayer, miracles, or
  moral freedom.
- Fine-tuning arguments require careful probability assumptions and must face
  multiverse, selection-effect, necessity, and unknown-law counterarguments.
- Emergence and consciousness are important research areas, but they do not by
  themselves establish a divine pattern.

Validity rule:

Science can support a modest claim: reality is deeply ordered, mathematically
describable, probabilistic at quantum scales, and open to philosophical
interpretation. It cannot be used as shortcut proof for theology.
