# Source-Specific History And Memory Cases

Source lane: history
Source status: cross-layer case expansion

These cases keep history from becoming a smooth story of progress. They ask
whether the pattern survives violence, institutional failure, contested memory,
and absent repair.

## Fourth-Century Trinitarian Controversy

- Historical anchor: Nicene and post-Nicene disputes over the Son, Spirit,
  worship, and imperial/church politics.
- Pattern signal: doctrinal language developed under conflict and worship
  pressure, not abstract speculation alone.
- Counter-reading: political power and ecclesial exclusion complicate any
  simple story of doctrinal clarity.
- Allowed use: support cross-era doctrine only with conflict, reception, and
  dissent named.

## Reformation And Confessional Conflict

- Historical anchor: sixteenth-century disputes over grace, authority, church,
  sacraments, and reform.
- Pattern signal: grace and scripture can generate renewal and critique.
- Counter-reading: reform also produced fragmentation, coercion, and political
  violence.
- Allowed use: treat reform as pressure test, not automatic proof of
  purification.

## Colonization And Mission

- Historical anchor: missionary expansion entangled with land, language, race,
  empire, and education.
- Pattern signal: translation and witness can cross cultures.
- Counter-reading: Christian language can be joined to domination, erasure, and
  forced assimilation.
- Allowed use: no universal claim should be strengthened without colonial and
  Indigenous counter-readings.

## Public Memory After Atrocity

- Historical anchor: museums, testimony archives, truth commissions, memorials,
  and contested monuments.
- Pattern signal: truth-telling and memory can resist denial.
- Counter-reading: memory institutions can become symbolic repair while
  survivors remain uncompensated or unheard.
- Allowed use: memory supports the pattern only when it remains accountable to
  harmed communities.
