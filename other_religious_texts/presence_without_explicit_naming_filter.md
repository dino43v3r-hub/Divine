# Presence Without Explicit Naming Filter

Source lane: other religious texts
Source status: comparative theology guardrail

The project needs a way to ask whether Jesus may be present and whether the
Holy Ghost may be working where Christian names are absent, without forcing
other religions into Christian categories.

## Core Distinction

- Christian interpretation: a Christian may interpret goodness, truth, witness,
  conviction, beauty, mercy, or transformation as signs compatible with Christ
  and the Holy Ghost.
- Other tradition self-understanding: the tradition may explain those same
  realities through its own theology, philosophy, practice, or community memory.

Both must be recorded.

## Possible Signals To Study

- unexpected attraction to Jesus,
- dreams or visions involving Jesus,
- conviction of sin or need for mercy,
- experiences interpreted by Christians as the Holy Ghost drawing, convicting,
  comforting, or illuminating,
- movement toward forgiveness with justice,
- costly truth-telling,
- transformation toward love, courage, and service,
- encounter with scripture or Christian witness,
- long-term discipleship after conversion.

## Counter-Readings

- Shared human moral experience.
- Psychological crisis or coping.
- Social influence or media exposure.
- Similarity of religious symbols across cultures.
- Selective reporting of dramatic conversion stories.
- Christian projection onto non-Christian sources.

## Required Claim Language

Use:

- "Christian interpretation may read this as..."
- "This testimony raises a research question..."
- "This source shows resonance, not proof..."
- "The tradition's own explanation is..."

Avoid:

- "This proves Jesus was hidden in..."
- "This religion secretly teaches..."
- "All traditions are really saying..."
- "The Holy Ghost is present because the pattern says so..."

## Analyzer Rule

Presence without explicit naming is a discernment question, not an automatic
classification. A claim must preserve the other tradition's own account and the
Christian account side by side until strong source review justifies a narrower
conclusion.
