# Spiritual Gifts And Comparative Discernment

Source lane: other religious texts
Source status: comparative pneumatology guardrail

Many religions and cultures describe extraordinary experiences: healing,
dreams, visions, prophecy, spirit possession, ecstatic speech, wisdom,
discernment, moral transformation, ritual power, and compassion. The divine
pattern should not ignore these. It should test them carefully.

## Christian Question

If the gifts of the Holy Ghost are part of God's action, then the project should
look for how God may be drawing, healing, convicting, warning, comforting, or
transforming people across countries and peoples.

But Christian discernment must distinguish:

- gifts of the Holy Spirit,
- natural human capacities,
- psychological or social dynamics,
- cultural ritual forms,
- other religions' own spiritual explanations,
- deception, coercion, or abuse,
- unknown or mixed cases.

## Traditions To Study Respectfully

- Islamic dreams, healing, prayer, and guidance traditions.
- Jewish prophecy, wisdom, prayer, and Spirit/breath language.
- Hindu bhakti, darshan, healing, devotion, and guru-disciple contexts.
- Buddhist compassion, insight, dreams, monastic discipline, and healing
  narratives.
- Sikh seva, prayer, devotion, and communal service.
- Indigenous healing, ancestor, land, vision, and ceremony traditions.
- Folk religions, new religious movements, and secular spiritual experiences.

## Allowed Christian Interpretation

- "A Christian may discern resonance with the Holy Ghost's work here."
- "This resembles a gift-pattern but needs source review."
- "This testimony may show God drawing a person before explicit Christian
  naming."
- "This should be compared with biblical fruit, doctrine, and community
  discernment."

## Not Allowed

- Do not call every healing or vision a gift of the Holy Spirit.
- Do not erase the tradition's own explanation.
- Do not use gifts to claim superiority over other peoples.
- Do not treat spectacular experiences as stronger than love, truth, justice,
  and holiness.
- Do not ignore abuse, fraud, coercion, or social pressure.

## Analyzer Rule

The pattern may count global gift-like experiences only as candidate signals
until they pass doctrine, fruit, community, justice, psychology, anthropology,
and comparative-religion filters.
