# Comparative Sacred Text Notes

Source lane: other religious texts
Source status: summarized comparison, counter-reading

This lane compares non-Christian sacred and wisdom traditions on their own
terms. It may include Torah and rabbinic interpretation, Quran and tafsir,
Hindu Veda, Upanishad, Bhagavad Gita, Buddhist sutra and Tripitaka traditions,
Daoist and Confucian classics, Sikh Guru Granth Sahib, Indigenous oral
tradition, and other sacred text or ritual communities when responsibly sourced.

Pattern observations:

- Many traditions describe order, law, wisdom, moral response, suffering,
  ritual, community, discipline, compassion, justice, and transformation.
- Shared language does not make the traditions the same. A Buddhist account of
  suffering, a Muslim account of submission to God, a Jewish account of covenant,
  a Hindu account of dharma, and an Indigenous account of land, ancestor, and
  ceremony each need their own context.
- These sources can test whether the project has found a broad human pattern or
  whether it is projecting Christian categories onto other communities.
- Counterargument: recurrence across religions may indicate shared human
  psychology, social formation, or moral imagination rather than direct support
  for a Trinitarian claim.

Validity rule:

Other religious texts can support the broad hypothesis that humans seek order,
meaning, moral repair, community, and transformation. They do not validate the
specifically Christian claim about Father, Son, and Holy Spirit unless Christian
sources and Trinitarian reasoning carry that distinct claim.
