# Divine Pattern Response Pressure Test

Type: psychology and cognition pressure test
Source status: behavioral guardrail

This test asks whether human reactions to a proposed divine pattern differ from
ordinary reactions to patterns.

## Claim Under Test

People may respond differently when they believe a pattern is divine rather
than merely interesting, beautiful, useful, or coincidental.

## Ordinary Pattern Reaction

- curiosity,
- prediction,
- pleasure,
- fear,
- agency detection,
- control-seeking,
- social sharing,
- overfitting,
- confirmation bias.

## Divine Pattern Reaction

- worship,
- repentance,
- prayer,
- testimony,
- obedience,
- moral repair,
- mercy,
- justice,
- humility,
- gifts of the Holy Ghost,
- courage under suffering,
- communal discernment.

## Pressure Questions

- Is the response explainable by ordinary pattern recognition?
- Is the person under stress, grief, danger, or social pressure?
- Is the pattern statistically meaningful or curated?
- Does the interpretation produce good fruit over time?
- Does it increase love and justice, or does it increase control and fear?
- Does the person become more truthful and humble, or more certain and
  coercive?
- Does the community test the pattern, or reward untested claims?

## Failure Conditions

The divine-pattern claim weakens if:

- noticing a pattern is treated as proof,
- divine language is used to bypass evidence,
- the response is mainly fear, status, control, or spectacle,
- negative cases are ignored,
- ordinary psychology explains the reaction well enough,
- there is no fruit of love, truth, humility, justice, or service.

## Result Rule

The difference is not merely intensity. A divine-pattern response should be
tested by fruit, doctrine, evidence, humility, community discernment, and
resistance to abuse.
