# Absent-Repair Extreme Pressure Cases

Type: hard unresolved-suffering pressure tests
Source status: practical case framework, counterargument

These cases intensify the unresolved-suffering lane. They are not decorative
examples. They are tests that can weaken the pattern.

## Disappeared Person With No Body Returned

- Pressure: a family never receives the body, confession, trial, or clear truth.
- Pattern risk: memory and hope can be spoken too cheaply.
- Counter-readings: state violence, war, organized crime, migration systems,
  racism, and bureaucratic indifference.
- Required response: say repair is absent. At most, name witness, lament,
  advocacy, and refusal to forget.

## Child Harmed By Preventable Institutional Failure

- Pressure: a child suffers or dies because authorities ignore warnings.
- Pattern risk: providence language can sound monstrous.
- Counter-readings: negligence, poverty, racism, family systems, policy,
  overloaded care systems, and institutional self-protection.
- Required response: strengthen justice and prevention language; weaken any
  claim that implies hidden purpose.

## Public Lie That Permanently Destroys A Life

- Pressure: reputation, employment, family, or safety is lost because a false
  narrative becomes socially fixed.
- Pattern risk: truth does not visibly triumph.
- Counter-readings: media incentives, group identity, scapegoating, legal
  weakness, and social contagion.
- Required response: truth and witness remain good, but the pattern cannot
  claim completed repair.

## Community Betrayed By A False Spiritual Gift

- Pressure: prophecy, healing claims, or spiritual authority mislead vulnerable
  people.
- Pattern risk: gifts language becomes abuse.
- Counter-readings: charisma, suggestibility, social pressure, fraud, trauma,
  and desire for certainty.
- Required response: claimed gifts must be tested by fruit, truth, doctrine,
  accountability, and protection of the vulnerable.

## Generational Loss Where Later Good Does Not Undo Harm

- Pressure: later descendants find strength, but the original loss remains
  unreturned and uncompensated.
- Pattern risk: resilience is mistaken for repair.
- Counter-readings: colonial theft, slavery, displacement, poverty, land loss,
  cultural erasure, and inherited trauma.
- Required response: distinguish survival, witness, partial healing, and actual
  restitution.
