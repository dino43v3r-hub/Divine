# Source-Specific Visual Art Case Notes

Source lane: visual art
Source status: cross-layer case expansion

These notes add concrete visual cases for cross-layer synthesis. They do not
claim that art proves theology. They ask how form, history, language, trauma,
and worship pressure-test a proposed pattern.

## Crucifixion And Lament Images

- Visual anchor: crucifixion, deposition, and lamentation scenes in Christian
  art.
- Cross-layer routes: theologians, history, psychology, human stories,
  unresolved suffering.
- Pattern signal: the image can hold divine love, bodily suffering, public
  shame, grief, and witness in one visual field.
- Counter-reading: crucifixion art can aestheticize pain, normalize passive
  endurance, or be used to tell victims to suffer silently.
- Revision rule: if the image is used without justice, bodily harm, and
  historical violence, weaken the pattern.

## Byzantine Icon Of The Trinity / Hospitality Of Abraham

- Visual anchor: icon traditions connected to Genesis 18 and Trinitarian
  reception.
- Cross-layer routes: biblical languages, all texts, theologians, worship.
- Pattern signal: relation, communion, hospitality, and mystery can be held in
  visual form.
- Counter-reading: later Trinitarian interpretation should not erase the
  Hebrew narrative context of Abrahamic hospitality.
- Revision rule: keep canonical reception and original narrative context
  distinct.

## Guernica As Secular Lament

- Visual anchor: Picasso's *Guernica* as a modern image of violence and grief.
- Cross-layer routes: history, politics, psychology, human stories, all texts.
- Pattern signal: non-religious art can carry lament, protest, fragmentation,
  and moral witness.
- Counter-reading: secular lament may expose human truth without supporting a
  specifically Trinitarian claim.
- Revision rule: treat recurrence as broad human pattern unless Christian
  source-specific evidence is also present.

## Memorial And Ruin Art

- Visual anchor: memorials, ruins, absence, empty chairs, names, damaged
  objects, and public grief practices.
- Cross-layer routes: history memory, unresolved suffering, psychology,
  cultural inputs.
- Pattern signal: memory and witness can resist erasure when repair is absent.
- Counter-reading: memorial form can become symbolic closure before material
  justice has occurred.
- Revision rule: do not call memory "redemption" unless repair, truth, and
  accountability are addressed.
