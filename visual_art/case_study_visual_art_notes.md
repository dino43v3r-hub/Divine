# Case Study Visual Art Notes

Source lane: visual art
Source status: case-study expansion

These notes add concrete visual-art cases for cross-layer synthesis. They do
not reproduce images; they describe interpretive questions to apply when an
actual artwork is reviewed.

## Crucifixion Images

Pattern signal:

- Body, wound, posture, gaze, darkness, crowd, and exposed vulnerability connect
  meaning, suffering, injustice, and possible hope.

Pressure:

- Crucifixion art can comfort the suffering, but it can also aestheticize pain.
  The pattern fails if beauty makes violence look spiritually useful or morally
  simple.

## Icon Of The Trinity / Hospitality Of Abraham

Pattern signal:

- Table, hospitality, mutual gaze, symmetry, and open space can suggest
  communion and invitation.

Pressure:

- Icon interpretation must respect liturgical and historical context. The image
  is not a generic diagram of relationship.

## Memorial Art

Pattern signal:

- Names, absence, repeated forms, empty space, public location, and ritual
  visitation connect memory, grief, justice, and communal practice.

Pressure:

- Memorial art often refuses closure. The pattern should preserve memory rather
  than force transformation.

## Protest Art

Pattern signal:

- Scale, public placement, disrupted symbols, faces, bodies, text, and color can
  reveal injustice and call for moral response.

Pressure:

- Protest art may indict religious communities. The pattern fails if it
  interprets protest as disorder instead of listening for truth.
