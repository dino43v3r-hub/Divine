# Cross-Era Primary Source Expansion

Type: theologian source material expansion
Source status: primary/classical source map, review notes

This file adds more actual theologian source material across eras. It is a
source map and interpretation guide, not a claim that all theologians say the
same thing.

## Patristic

- Irenaeus, *Against Heresies*: creation is good, redemption is not escape from
  creation, and recapitulation in Christ frames repair as restoration and
  fulfillment.
- Athanasius, *On the Incarnation*: the Son/Logos is central to creation,
  revelation, and redemption; the pattern must keep Christology stronger than a
  vague meaning principle.
- Basil, *On the Holy Spirit*: Spirit language should preserve worship,
  sanctification, communion, and divine agency rather than reducing the Spirit
  to emotion or group energy.
- Augustine, *Confessions* and *De Trinitate*: desire, memory, restlessness,
  love, and Trinitarian reasoning must be handled with humility and analogy
  limits.

## Medieval

- Anselm, *Cur Deus Homo*: redemption, justice, and satisfaction raise questions
  about moral order and repair, but the model should not flatten atonement into
  a single theory.
- Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae*: creation, providence, participation, virtue,
  natural law, grace, and beatitude offer a disciplined metaphysical vocabulary.
- Bonaventure, *The Journey of the Mind to God*: creation, sign, beauty, ascent,
  and Christ-centered contemplation connect order and meaning without making
  nature self-sufficient.
- Julian of Norwich, *Revelations of Divine Love*: suffering, divine love, hope,
  and unresolved anguish require patience; do not turn "all shall be well" into
  denial of grief.

## Reformation

- Luther, *The Freedom of a Christian* and the Heidelberg Disputation:
  righteousness, cross, hiddenness, faith, and vocation challenge triumphal
  patterns.
- Calvin, *Institutes*: creation, providence, scripture, union with Christ,
  Spirit, and church discipline should be tested against pastoral misuse and
  institutional power.

## Modern And Contemporary

- Barth, *Church Dogmatics*: revelation in Christ resists reducing God to a
  general religious pattern.
- Bonhoeffer, *Discipleship* and prison writings: costly grace, suffering,
  public responsibility, and life before God test whether transformation is
  embodied.
- Moltmann, *The Crucified God*: the pattern must face suffering from within
  Christian theology, not rush to resolution.
- James Cone, *The Cross and the Lynching Tree*: any divine pattern that cannot
  confront racial terror, power, and public injustice is morally incomplete.
- Sarah Coakley, *God, Sexuality, and the Self*: prayer, desire, vulnerability,
  and Spirit-led transformation resist control-oriented theology.
- Willie James Jennings, *The Christian Imagination*: race, land, belonging,
  colonial history, and distorted Christian identity are central pressure tests.

## Pattern Rule

Cross-era agreement can strengthen a Christian theological pattern. Cross-era
disagreement can also strengthen the method by forcing clearer limits. The
pattern should be revised whenever a theologian exposes overclaim, misuse,
weak Christology, weak pneumatology, or neglect of suffering and justice.
