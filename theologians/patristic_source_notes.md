# Patristic Source Notes

Source lane: theologians
Era: Patristic
Source status: source reviewed

These notes add actual patristic voices as pattern-design material. They are
not a substitute for reading the full sources, but they identify claims the app
should test against the wider corpus.

## Irenaeus

Primary source focus: *Against Heresies*.

Pattern contribution: creation, incarnation, recapitulation, and salvation are
held together. The Son does not merely repair an isolated moral problem; Christ
gathers human history back toward God. This helps test whether a divine pattern
can move from creation to fall, embodiment, redemption, and restored communion.

Risk: do not flatten Irenaeus into a modern progress story. Recapitulation is
Christ-centered and ecclesial, not generic human improvement.

## Athanasius

Primary source focus: *On the Incarnation*.

Pattern contribution: the Word/Logos through whom creation is made enters
creation to heal corruption and restore knowledge of God. This strongly supports
a layer movement from order and Logos to incarnation, healing, and
transformation.

Risk: do not reduce incarnation to a symbol of meaning. Athanasius treats it as
God's concrete saving action in Christ.

## Basil And The Cappadocians

Primary source focus: Basil, Gregory of Nazianzus, Gregory of Nyssa.

Pattern contribution: Trinitarian distinction and unity are guarded carefully.
The Father, Son, and Holy Spirit cannot be collapsed into interchangeable
functions. Any pattern that says "source, meaning, transformation" must still
preserve personal distinction, worship, and communion.

Risk: a pattern fails if it turns the Trinity into psychology, social theory, or
mere metaphor.

## Augustine

Primary source focus: *Confessions* and *On the Trinity*.

Pattern contribution: desire, memory, restlessness, sin, grace, and love form a
deep anthropology. Augustine helps connect psychology, longing, moral disorder,
conversion, and worship without reducing grace to technique.

Risk: desire must be examined truthfully, including disordered love, pride, and
self-deception.
