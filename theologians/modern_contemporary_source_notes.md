# Modern And Contemporary Source Notes

Source lane: theologians
Era: Modern and Contemporary
Source status: source reviewed

## Karl Barth

Primary source focus: *Church Dogmatics*.

Pattern contribution: revelation is centered in God's self-disclosure in Jesus
Christ. Barth resists making theology rest on general religious feeling or
natural patterns alone. This is a major guardrail for the analyzer.

Risk: cross-layer patterns must remain answerable to revelation and not become a
self-authorizing system.

## Dietrich Bonhoeffer

Primary source focus: *Discipleship*, *Life Together*, prison writings.

Pattern contribution: Christology, community, costly discipleship, prayer, and
public responsibility belong together. Bonhoeffer tests whether a pattern can
survive political evil, prison, silence, and moral cost.

Risk: do not admire costly discipleship abstractly while ignoring concrete
neighbors, risk, and obedience.

## Jurgen Moltmann

Primary source focus: *The Crucified God* and theology of hope.

Pattern contribution: suffering and hope must be interpreted through the cross
and resurrection. God is not distant from suffering; hope is eschatological, not
simple optimism.

Risk: hope must not erase lament, grief, or the scandal of evil.

## James Cone

Primary source focus: Black liberation theology and the cross.

Pattern contribution: any pattern that ignores racial terror, oppression,
liberation, and public justice is theologically fragile. The cross must be
tested against victims of history, not only private spirituality.

Risk: do not universalize suffering in a way that erases concrete oppressed
communities.

## Sarah Coakley, Rowan Williams, N. T. Wright, Willie James Jennings

Pattern contribution: prayer, desire, scripture, resurrection, race, church,
embodiment, and public life deepen the analyzer's ability to connect theology
with actual human formation.

Risk: contemporary synthesis should preserve disagreement and source context
instead of forcing every theologian into one voice.
