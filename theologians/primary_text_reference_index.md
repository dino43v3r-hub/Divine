# Primary Text Reference Index

Source lane: theologians
Source status: primary-text reference index

This index adds more precise primary-text anchors for cross-era theologian
review. It is a guide for checking sources, not a substitute for reading the
texts directly.

## Patristic

### Irenaeus

- Primary anchor: *Against Heresies*, Book III on apostolic witness and rule of
  faith; Book V on recapitulation and embodied salvation.
- Pattern check: creation and redemption are connected through Christ.
- Disagreement pressure: anti-gnostic polemic means the source should be read as
  a contested argument, not neutral system-building.

### Athanasius

- Primary anchor: *On the Incarnation*, sections on the Word, creation,
  corruption, and restoration; anti-Arian writings on the Son's relation to the
  Father.
- Pattern check: the Word is not merely rational structure but the Son.
- Disagreement pressure: any pattern that turns Logos into abstract order fails
  Athanasius' Christological test.

### Basil

- Primary anchor: *On the Holy Spirit*, arguments from baptism, doxology, and
  worship.
- Pattern check: the Spirit is confessed through worship and divine action.
- Disagreement pressure: the Spirit cannot be reduced to human transformation,
  emotion, or social energy.

### Augustine

- Primary anchor: *Confessions*, especially memory, desire, restlessness, and
  conversion; *De Trinitate* on analogy and the limits of mental images.
- Pattern check: desire and memory can be interpreted theologically.
- Disagreement pressure: psychological analogy for the Trinity is limited and
  must not replace doctrine or worship.

## Medieval

### Anselm

- Primary anchor: *Cur Deus Homo* on satisfaction, sin, justice, and fittingness.
- Pattern check: repair cannot bypass moral seriousness.
- Disagreement pressure: atonement cannot be reduced to a single model or used
  to ignore liberation, healing, and resurrection.

### Aquinas

- Primary anchor: *Summa Theologiae* I on God, creation, providence, and
  Trinity; I-II and II-II on virtue and human action.
- Pattern check: nature, reason, grace, and virtue can be ordered without being
  collapsed.
- Disagreement pressure: analogy and scope matter; metaphysical order is not
  permission to explain away suffering.

### Julian of Norwich

- Primary anchor: *Revelations of Divine Love* on sin, suffering, divine love,
  and hope.
- Pattern check: hope is spoken from inside suffering.
- Disagreement pressure: "all shall be well" is not allowed as premature
  closure.

## Reformation

### Luther

- Primary anchor: Heidelberg Disputation; *The Freedom of a Christian*.
- Pattern check: cross, grace, freedom, and service belong together.
- Disagreement pressure: visible success, order, and power can hide the cross.

### Calvin

- Primary anchor: *Institutes*, Book I on knowledge of God and providence; Book
  III on Spirit, faith, repentance, and Christian life.
- Pattern check: providence and Spirit-witness can shape life.
- Disagreement pressure: providence language must not become a quick answer to
  trauma, injustice, or unanswered prayer.

## Modern And Contemporary

### Barth

- Primary anchor: *Church Dogmatics* on revelation and election; Barmen
  Declaration on political idolatry.
- Pattern check: Christ judges human religion, culture, and natural theology.
- Disagreement pressure: broad patterns from nature or culture cannot bypass
  revelation in Christ.

### Bonhoeffer

- Primary anchor: *Discipleship*, *Life Together*, prison letters.
- Pattern check: costly grace and communal life are tested under suffering and
  political evil.
- Disagreement pressure: theology that avoids public responsibility is
  weakened.

### Moltmann

- Primary anchor: *The Crucified God* and *Theology of Hope*.
- Pattern check: hope must pass through cross and lament.
- Disagreement pressure: optimism, progress, and beauty cannot replace
  cruciform hope.

### Cone

- Primary anchor: *A Black Theology of Liberation* and *The Cross and the
  Lynching Tree*.
- Pattern check: cross and liberation must face racial terror.
- Disagreement pressure: universal suffering language is weakened if it hides
  concrete oppression.

### Coakley

- Primary anchor: *God, Sexuality, and the Self*.
- Pattern check: desire, prayer, gender, and Spirit require contemplative and
  ascetic discernment.
- Disagreement pressure: experience is not automatically Spirit; desire must be
  tested.

### Jennings

- Primary anchor: *The Christian Imagination*.
- Pattern check: Christian identity is shaped by land, race, belonging, and
  colonial history.
- Disagreement pressure: universal claims are weakened when they ignore
  geography, displacement, and racial formation.

## Use Rule

No theologian source should be promoted to strong evidence until the project
can name the primary text, the narrow claim it supports, the disagreement it
introduces, and the way it pressures the current pattern.
