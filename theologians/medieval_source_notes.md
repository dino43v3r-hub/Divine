# Medieval Source Notes

Source lane: theologians
Era: Medieval
Source status: source reviewed

## Anselm

Primary source focus: *Proslogion* and *Cur Deus Homo*.

Pattern contribution: faith seeks understanding. Reason is not opposed to
worship; it is drawn into prayer, humility, and contemplation. This supports the
app's layer from meaning and reason toward worship, while also warning against
reason detached from reverence.

Risk: do not treat rational coherence as proof that bypasses faith,
scripture, or lived obedience.

## Aquinas

Primary source focus: *Summa Theologiae*.

Pattern contribution: creation, being, causality, finality, virtue, grace, and
sacrament can be studied in an ordered way. Aquinas helps the analyzer separate
levels of explanation: natural causes, metaphysical dependence, theological
revelation, moral virtue, and grace.

Risk: avoid using Aquinas as a shortcut from natural order to total doctrinal
certainty. He distinguishes what reason can know from what revelation gives.

## Bonaventure

Primary source focus: *The Soul's Journey into God*.

Pattern contribution: creation is read as a trace of God that draws the mind
toward contemplation. Beauty, order, intellect, desire, and love become a path of
ascent.

Risk: contemplative symbolism must be tested against humility, charity, and the
cross.

## Julian Of Norwich

Primary source focus: *Revelations of Divine Love*.

Pattern contribution: suffering, sin, divine love, and hope are held together
without making pain trivial. Julian is useful for unresolved suffering because
she can speak of trust and hope while still taking anguish seriously.

Risk: do not use "all shall be well" as an easy closure over trauma or injustice.
