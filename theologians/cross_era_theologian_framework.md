Title: Cross-Era Theologian Framework
Type: Initial pattern-design seed

Patristic voices such as Irenaeus, Athanasius, Basil, Gregory of Nazianzus, Gregory of Nyssa, and Augustine help guard the Trinity, creation, incarnation, and salvation.
Medieval voices such as Anselm, Aquinas, Bonaventure, Julian of Norwich, and Catherine of Siena develop creation, grace, virtue, suffering, and divine love.
Reformation voices such as Luther and Calvin sharpen grace, scripture, church, worship, and Christology.
Modern voices such as Schleiermacher, Kierkegaard, Newman, Barth, Bonhoeffer, Rahner, Moltmann, and von Balthasar deepen revelation, suffering, public discipleship, Trinity, and hope.
Contemporary voices such as James Cone, Sarah Coakley, Rowan Williams, N. T. Wright, Willie James Jennings, and Beth Felker Jones bring justice, embodiment, church, race, public life, and transformation into the pattern conversation.

Pattern design should not flatten these theologians into one voice.
It should ask where they agree, where they disagree, and whether a proposed pattern survives across era, tradition, suffering, church practice, and justice.

The Trinitarian guardrail remains central: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are distinct persons and one God.
Creation is from the Father, revelation and redemption are centered in the Son / Logos, and communion and sanctification are made present by the Holy Spirit.
