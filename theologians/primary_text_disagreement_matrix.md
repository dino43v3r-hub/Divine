# Primary Text Disagreement Matrix

Source lane: theologians
Source status: primary-text review map

This matrix deepens the theologian lane by naming primary works, the pattern
signal each source may support, and the disagreement or pressure point that
prevents flattening the theologians into one voice.

## Patristic

### Irenaeus

- Primary text anchor: *Against Heresies*, especially the rule-of-faith and
  recapitulation arguments.
- Possible pattern signal: creation, incarnation, and restoration are held
  together rather than treated as separate religious ideas.
- Disagreement or pressure: Irenaeus is combating gnostic cosmologies, so his
  argument should not be lifted out of its polemical context as a generic
  theory of all reality.
- Allowed use: support a cautious claim that early Christian theology connects
  creation, Christ, embodied salvation, and divine purpose.

### Athanasius

- Primary text anchor: *On the Incarnation* and anti-Arian works.
- Possible pattern signal: the Word who creates is the Word who redeems.
- Disagreement or pressure: Athanasius is defending Nicene Christology, not
  offering a general philosophy of patterns. Weak Christology would break the
  project rather than strengthen it.
- Allowed use: support the Christian-specific claim that Logos language must be
  tied to the person of the Son, not abstract order alone.

### Basil of Caesarea

- Primary text anchor: *On the Holy Spirit*.
- Possible pattern signal: worship, doxology, and Spirit language help preserve
  Trinitarian distinction and unity.
- Disagreement or pressure: arguments about liturgical practice and doxology
  cannot be reduced to psychological transformation language.
- Allowed use: support a guardrail that the Spirit cannot be treated merely as
  human experience or community energy.

## Medieval

### Anselm

- Primary text anchor: *Cur Deus Homo*.
- Possible pattern signal: sin, justice, satisfaction, and divine mercy are
  treated as morally serious.
- Disagreement or pressure: satisfaction logic can be read in ways that narrow
  atonement or underplay liberation, healing, and resurrection.
- Allowed use: support a limited claim that repair must face moral disorder,
  not bypass it.

### Aquinas

- Primary text anchor: *Summa Theologiae*, especially questions on God,
  creation, providence, virtue, and the Trinity.
- Possible pattern signal: created order, reason, virtue, and grace can be
  related without collapsing nature into grace.
- Disagreement or pressure: scholastic synthesis can look too tidy when placed
  next to unresolved suffering, historical violence, and modern suspicion of
  total systems.
- Allowed use: support disciplined metaphysical language only when matched with
  humility about scope and analogy.

### Julian of Norwich

- Primary text anchor: *Revelations of Divine Love*.
- Possible pattern signal: divine love is spoken into suffering rather than away
  from suffering.
- Disagreement or pressure: "all shall be well" must not be used as a shortcut
  over grief, abuse, or injustice.
- Allowed use: support hope under lament, not forced resolution.

## Reformation

### Luther

- Primary text anchor: Heidelberg Disputation and *The Freedom of a Christian*.
- Possible pattern signal: cross, grace, freedom, and service are bound
  together.
- Disagreement or pressure: the theology of the cross challenges any pattern
  that depends on visible success, beauty, power, or institutional triumph.
- Allowed use: weaken claims that infer divine pattern from prosperity or
  coherence alone.

### Calvin

- Primary text anchor: *Institutes of the Christian Religion*, especially
  providence, knowledge of God, and Spirit-witness themes.
- Possible pattern signal: creation, providence, scripture, and human response
  are integrated.
- Disagreement or pressure: providence language can become pastorally dangerous
  if it explains suffering too quickly or ignores secondary causes.
- Allowed use: support careful providence language only with suffering and
  justice guardrails attached.

## Modern And Contemporary

### Barth

- Primary text anchor: *Church Dogmatics* and the Barmen Declaration.
- Possible pattern signal: revelation in Christ judges natural theology and
  political idolatry.
- Disagreement or pressure: Barth sharply limits attempts to read God directly
  from culture, nature, or history.
- Allowed use: require Christological discipline before strengthening broad
  pattern claims.

### Bonhoeffer

- Primary text anchor: *Discipleship*, *Life Together*, and prison writings.
- Possible pattern signal: costly grace, embodied obedience, community, and
  suffering confront cheap religious certainty.
- Disagreement or pressure: theology must be tested under political violence
  and institutional compromise.
- Allowed use: weaken any pattern that cannot name complicity, cost, and public
  responsibility.

### Moltmann

- Primary text anchor: *The Crucified God* and *Theology of Hope*.
- Possible pattern signal: hope is credible only through the crucified God, not
  through optimism.
- Disagreement or pressure: suffering is not a decorative theme; it is a
  theological crisis.
- Allowed use: strengthen only claims that keep lament, protest, and eschatology
  together.

### James Cone

- Primary text anchor: *A Black Theology of Liberation* and *The Cross and the
  Lynching Tree*.
- Possible pattern signal: cross-language must face racial terror and liberation
  rather than abstract suffering.
- Disagreement or pressure: white or dominant theological systems may hide
  oppression inside universal language.
- Allowed use: require social location, power, and harmed communities before
  promoting claims about suffering or redemption.

### Sarah Coakley

- Primary text anchor: *God, Sexuality, and the Self*.
- Possible pattern signal: prayer, desire, gender, and Trinity require ascetic
  and contemplative discipline.
- Disagreement or pressure: desire is not automatically holy; it must be
  discerned, purified, and socially tested.
- Allowed use: support careful pneumatology of transformation while resisting
  vague experience claims.

### Willie James Jennings

- Primary text anchor: *The Christian Imagination*.
- Possible pattern signal: Christian identity, land, race, and belonging are
  historically entangled.
- Disagreement or pressure: mission, doctrine, and belonging can be distorted by
  colonial formation.
- Allowed use: weaken any universal pattern claim that lacks colonial,
  racialized, and geographic counter-readings.

## Rule For The Analyzer

The pattern is stronger only when it survives named disagreement. If a source
exposes weak Christology, weak pneumatology, coerced forgiveness, colonial
formation, political idolatry, or unresolved suffering, the pattern should be
revised before it is strengthened.
