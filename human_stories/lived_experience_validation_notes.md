# Lived Experience Validation Notes

Source lane: human stories
Source status: practical lived source, counter-reading

Use human stories as practical witnesses: interviews, testimony, pastoral
encounters, memoir summaries, counseling observations, recovery narratives,
community conflict, grief, illness, vocation, repair, and reconciliation. Protect
privacy and avoid identifying details unless a story is already public and
properly sourced.

Pattern observations:

- Human stories often include a starting order, disruption, suffering, confusion,
  moral choice, help from others, embodied practice, and some form of repair or
  transformation.
- Some stories do not resolve. Chronic illness, injustice, abuse, poverty, war,
  grief, and unanswered prayer may leave the pattern incomplete.
- Secular counseling, recovery groups, family care, medicine, labor organizing,
  and friendship can produce real healing without explicit Christian language.
- Counterargument: lived transformation may be explained by attachment, habit,
  medicine, community support, justice work, or social change.

Validity rule:

Human stories validate practical usefulness only when the pattern helps people
tell the truth, resist harm, seek justice, receive care, and practice repair. It
fails if it pressures people to call unresolved pain a completed transformation.
