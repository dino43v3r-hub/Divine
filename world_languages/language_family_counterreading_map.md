# Language Family Counter-Reading Map

Source lane: world languages
Source status: mapped coverage with limits

The project maps language families as a research agenda, not as universal
evidence. A family or tradition should not count as covered until there are
source-specific notes, translator choices, context, and rival readings.

## Indo-European

- Sampling need: Greek, Latin, German, English, Sanskrit, Persian, and modern
  vernacular sources should not be treated as interchangeable.
- Counter-reading: European Christian vocabulary may dominate the project and
  hide other conceptual structures.

## Afro-Asiatic

- Sampling need: Biblical Hebrew, Aramaic, Arabic, Ge'ez, and related
  traditions require distinct genre and community contexts.
- Counter-reading: shared Semitic roots do not automatically produce shared
  theological conclusions.

## Niger-Congo

- Sampling need: African Christian, oral, proverbial, liturgical, and
  postcolonial sources should be treated on their own terms.
- Counter-reading: translation through colonial mission languages may distort
  local categories of personhood, spirit, kinship, and land.

## Sino-Tibetan

- Sampling need: Chinese philosophical, poetic, ritual, and Christian
  translation sources require attention to terms for way, heaven, spirit, and
  moral cultivation.
- Counter-reading: conceptual resonance with order or wisdom is not evidence of
  Trinitarian structure by itself.

## Austronesian And Indigenous Languages

- Sampling need: oral tradition, land relation, ancestor memory, navigation,
  kinship, and ritual must be preserved as context.
- Counter-reading: reducing oral or Indigenous traditions to examples of a
  preselected Christian pattern repeats extraction.

## Analyzer Rule

Coverage language should say "mapped," "sampled," or "source-specific." It
should not say "universal" unless broad, multilingual, tradition-sensitive
source notes and counter-readings exist.
